# How AI Is Changing Entry-Level Jobs, Career Ladders, and Organizational Structure in Professional Services

*Research summary covering consulting, law, accounting, and finance. Compiled October 9, 2026.*

## Summary

- Early-career workers in AI-exposed occupations are losing ground, while senior hiring holds up.
- Big Four and consulting firms are cutting graduate intake, especially in the UK and in US audit.
- The junior roles that remain ask for more. Postings increasingly list mid- and senior-level skills such as judgement and leadership, and new hires are expected to direct and check AI output rather than produce first drafts.
- Firms disagree on where this is heading. IBM is tripling entry-level hiring, PwC UK expects graduate recruitment to recover, and EY and KPMG are redesigning the pipeline instead of simply shrinking it.
- Firms are reshaping from a pyramid into an obelisk or diamond: fewer juniors, who act as AI orchestrators and reviewers from day one.

## 1. The Evidence on Entry-Level Decline

### Stanford Digital Economy Lab
- Employment of workers aged 22-25 in AI-exposed occupations is **down 13-19% since 2022**.
- Senior hiring in the same occupations has **stayed strong**.

### UK finance entry-level listings (Businessday/Filed)
- Entry-level finance listings in the UK **fell 44% year on year**.
- Big Four graduate intake cuts:
  - KPMG: about -29%
  - Deloitte: about -18%
  - EY: about -11%
  - PwC: about -6%

### Financial Times analysis of 50,000 postings
- The Big Four now post **more AI-specialist roles than auditor roles**.

### Industry outlook
- **Dario Amodei (CEO, Anthropic):** up to **50% of entry-level roles in law, consulting, and finance** could disappear within one to five years.

## 2. Rising Skill Requirements for Junior Roles

### CMap research (October 8, 2026)
- **Two-thirds** of applicants for junior business consultant roles are now **seasoned professionals**.
- On average, **one in five** skills listed in junior postings is a senior-level skill.

### PwC study (June 2026)
- AI-exposed entry-level roles are **7x more likely** to require senior skills such as judgement and leadership.
- Junior postings that demand mid- or senior-level skills are **up 35% since 2019**.

## 3. Firm-by-Firm Responses

### McKinsey
- Workforce of about **40,000 humans and 25,000 AI agents**.
- Final-round candidates are tested on **directing and critiquing AI output**.

### PwC
- **US:** entry-level audit hiring is being cut **32-39% through 2028**.
- **Jenn Kosar (AI Assurance Leader):** new hires will do **manager-level work within three years**.
- **UK:** Marco Amitrano, PwC UK chief (October 7, 2026), said he expects graduate recruitment to **push back up**. UK entry-level numbers are broadly steady at **about 1,300**.

### KPMG
- In the UK, KPMG is hiring **as many apprentices as graduates** (September 27, 2026). Graduate intake is down about **29%**.
- **Marc Burrows (UK chief people officer):** graduates will **review and exercise judgement** rather than produce outputs, and professional exams are being front-loaded.
- KPMG rebuilt early-careers training for **about 1,000 interns** around **"think, prompt, check"**. Among those interns:
  - **57%** said direct coaching was most valuable.
  - **3%** valued AI-assisted learning most.
  - **43%** named over-reliance on AI limiting critical thinking as their top concern.

### EY
- **Career Residency** (launched August 2026) turns 8-week assurance internships into **8-12 month part-time engagements**. Successful participants join as **analysts rather than staff**.
- It drew **over 24,000 applications**, leading to **2,400 internship offers**.
- Graduate intake is down about **11%**.

### Deloitte
- **"The broken skills ladder"** (June 30, 2026, Deloitte Center for Government Insights): as AI automates foundational entry-level work, a gap opens between the expertise firms need and the pathways people use to build it.
- **Survey of 1,874 workers** (October 8, 2026):

| Belief | Early-career | Tenured |
|---|---|---|
| AI will create new opportunities | 75% | 58% |
| AI will help them move up | 77% | 56% |

### IBM (counterpoint)
- IBM is **tripling US entry-level hiring in 2026**. CHRO Nickle LaMoreaux announced this at Charter's Leading with AI Summit in February 2026.
- Entry-level roles have been rewritten:
  - Junior developers spend less time coding and more time with customers.
  - HR juniors handle the edge cases the chatbot can't.
- This reverses 2023, when CEO Arvind Krishna paused back-office hiring.

## 4. Career Ladders: What's Breaking and What's Replacing It

- **The bottom rung is thinning.** The routine drafting, reconciliation, and research that used to train juniors is increasingly automated (Deloitte, Stanford).
- **Juniors start higher up.** New hires are expected to review, judge, and direct AI output from the start (PwC, KPMG, McKinsey).
- **Pathways are being redesigned:**
  - apprenticeships (KPMG)
  - longer, residency-style internships (EY)
  - front-loaded professional exams (KPMG)
  - training built around "think, prompt, check" (KPMG)
- **Human coaching still matters most to juniors.** KPMG interns rated direct coaching far above AI-assisted learning and worried about over-reliance on AI.
- **Early-career workers are more optimistic than tenured staff** about AI's effect on their careers (Deloitte).

## 5. Organizational Structure: From Pyramid to Obelisk or Diamond

- In the traditional leverage model, a wide base of juniors supported fewer managers and partners.
- In the emerging **obelisk or diamond** shape, the base is narrower and juniors act as **AI orchestrators and reviewers from day one**.
- What this means for firms:
  - Leverage increasingly comes from AI agents rather than headcount (McKinsey's 25,000 agents).
  - Hiring tests shift toward critiquing and directing AI (McKinsey).
  - The long-term risk is a thin future bench of managers and partners if foundational experience is never built. This is the "broken skills ladder" Deloitte describes.
